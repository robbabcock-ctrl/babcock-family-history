"Fraternity, Charity and Loyalty" — an original essay written by Phoebe Ellen (McKinney) Keown
(1850-1934), widow of Civil War veteran Thomas H. Keown, published posthumously as a tribute
in the Arroyo Grande Valley Herald-Recorder, 11 January 1935, page 4, alongside a reprint of a
photograph of Thomas and Phoebe taken on their 50th wedding anniversary (1920).

The paper's own introduction: "Following is an article written by the late Mrs. Phoebe E. Keown,
aged 84 years, who passed away last week and who was the widow of a Veteran of the Grand Army
of the Republic. It is entitled 'Fraternity, Charity and Loyalty,' and is a beautiful word
description of the Grand Army's motto."

Full text, as transcribed from the newspaper scan:

"Born of battles and strengthened by the common suffering of years of war for the preservation
of the Union, the spirit of comradeship between the men who composed the immense armies of the
federal government from 1861 to 1865 has been strong enough to make the organization of the
Grand Army of the Republic, composed of Union Veterans, one that is in many respects the most
remarkable in history.

"Fraternity, Charity and Loyalty -- three words form the foundation on which the tenets of the
Grand Army of the Republic are based.

"Fraternity -- means the continuance of acquaintances made in camp, on the march and on the
fields of battle. A mantle of brotherly love thrown over a warm but fainting heart. A stay for
faltering steps, a smile for those who smile, a tear for those who weep and an ear and sympathy
for sorrow.

"Charity -- means faithful to promises to dying Comrades to care for the dear ones left behind.
And the Order is ever ready with the helping hand -- not the hand that bestows a tithe of wealth,
but, the widow's mite -- often their all. The Comrades, as a whole, are not blessed plentifully.
Their best life was given to their Country, and with no profession or trade, it is like gathering
fragments of the harvest where the reaper has spared little, but the fragments are divided and
the pledges carried out.

"Loyalty -- this crowning word means, when spoken from the Grand Army standpoint, that ALL that
the old soldiers fought for should be maintained. That our Country, great and good, shall be
represented from one end to the other by one flag -- the Stars and Stripes -- Old Glory."

--- Context ---
Phoebe died 31 December 1934 at her home on Allen Street, Arroyo Grande, at age 84 (born
16 July 1850, Burlington, Iowa). Her own obituary (Herald-Recorder, 4 Jan 1935, and Santa Maria
Times, 3 Jan 1935) announced this essay would follow the next week "as lack of space prevents it
being published this week" -- confirming this piece was written by Phoebe herself, in life, and
held for publication as a deliberate posthumous tribute rather than composed by the paper's staff.

The same 11 Jan 1935 issue reports that Colonel Harper Corps No. 33 (Women's Relief Corps, the
G.A.R. auxiliary Phoebe served as Past President) draped its charter in mourning "in memory of
their faithful and loyal member of many years, Mrs. Phoebe E. Keown."
