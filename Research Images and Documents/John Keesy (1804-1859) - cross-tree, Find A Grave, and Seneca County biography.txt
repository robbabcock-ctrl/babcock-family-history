Full title: "History of Seneca County, Ohio: Containing A History of the County, Its Townships,
Towns, Villages, Portraits of Early Settlers and Prominent Men, Biographies, History of the
Northwest Territory, History of Ohio, Statistical and Miscellaneous" -- p. 1028

NOTE: this is a DIFFERENT Ancestry collection ("U.S., Family History Books," collection 62282)
than the two HathiTrust volumes of a similarly-titled Seneca County history already checked
earlier in this project (where no David Ringle Sr. sketch was found) -- this one does carry a real
biographical entry for the Keesy family that was not previously located.

Full transcription (some words uncertain due to period typeface/OCR quality, marked [?]):

"REV. W. ALLEN KEESY, minister of the United Brethren Church, Attica, was born in Huron County,
Ohio, July 15, 1843; son of John and Elizabeth (Gouse) Keesy. [The name Keesy was originally
spelled Guesy.] His parents [were] of German origin, [and the family] moved with a family of ten
children from Huron County, Ohio, where the father, John Keesy, a farmer, resided for many years
and died in 1859. Our subject enlisted, in 1861, in Company I, Fifty-fifth Ohio Volunteer
Infantry, serving until 1862, and participated in the following battles and skirmishes: Bloomfield,
Franklin, Culpeper, Cross Keys, Strasburg and Bull Run, in Virginia, and was discharged on account
of disability, but, in 1864, he again enlisted in his country's service, serving as a
non-commissioned officer in the Sixty-fourth Ohio Volunteer Infantry, with which regiment he was
present at the battles of Spring Hill, Franklin, and two days at Nashville. He was reared on the
farm, attending the common schools, and, on his return from the army, [attended] the Normal
School at Milan, Erie Co., Ohio; commenced to teach school on his return from Milan, and taught
for five terms. He [became] a member of the United Brethren Church in 1864, [was] licensed to
exhort in 1867, was admitted to the annual conference in 1872, and regularly ordained in 1873. He
was pastor of the Huron Creek Circuit, then the Rocky Ridge, Republican, Genoa, and Norwalk
Center circuits were successively in charge, then transferred to Pontiac [?] and Clyde. He has
served the conference three years as presiding elder, two years of that time being spent in
Sandusky District. He was stationed at Attica in 1881. Rev. W. A. Keesy is an able speaker, [a]
concise [?] and diligent worker and faithful student. He has been twice married, first, in 1866,
to Margie J., daughter of Rev. B. T. Lane, a minister of the United Brethren Church. Of the three
children born to this union two are now living: Minerva and Mary. Mr. Keesy lost this wife by
death in 1873, and in 1874 he was married to Augusta Charles, by whom he has five children: Flora,
Dessie, Leona Silvesta [?], and Edith and Ethel (twins)."

NOTE: This substantially supplements the Rev. William Allen Keesy Find A Grave record already
saved earlier in this project (memorial 59698253) -- adding his full Civil War regimental detail
(55th and 64th Ohio Volunteer Infantry, specific battles), his ministry career (licensed 1867,
ordained 1873, multiple circuits), his time at Milan Normal School, and his two marriages (Margie
J. Lane, d. 1873; then Augusta Charles, 1874) with all 7 of his children named. Also confirms
father John Keesy died in 1859 as a farmer in Huron County, Ohio, consistent with the tree.
