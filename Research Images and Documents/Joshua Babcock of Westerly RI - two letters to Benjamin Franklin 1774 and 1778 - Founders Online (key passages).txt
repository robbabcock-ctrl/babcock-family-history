KEY PASSAGES (verbatim from the letters):
(1) Dateline "Westerly 15th Februa 1774", signed "Josh Babcock", addressed "Honble. Doctr. Franklin London". Mentions the Boston Tea Party ("the Fate of the India-Company's 342 Chests of Tea") and that "many of the Towns in the Colonys, in this in particular" were forming resolutions against dutied tea; also the Simsbury copper-mine prison.
(2) Dateline "Westerly 31 May 1778", signed "Josh Babcock", to "His Excellency Doctr Franklin". Describes Rhode Island under British occupation of Newport: "½ the Militia and ½ the Alarum-List have during the last Summer been constantly on Guard by Releifs till almost Christmass" (i.e., the Westerly alarm-list service that the SAR/DAR index credits to Oliver Babcock b. 1722, 'Reformados Alarm Men, Westerly, Capt. Bliven'); also the Feb 1778 Rhode Island act to enlist slaves.

EDITORIAL POINTS (paraphrase):
- Franklin Papers editors call him "the storekeeper and ex-physician of Westerly, R.I., with whom BF had been in intermittent correspondence since 1755", and say these are the only two surviving letters from him.
- Editors' note to the 1778 letter says a fuller sketch of him is in Stephen Babcock, Babcock Genealogy (New York, 1903), pp. 30-32 -- i.e., the same 1903 book already in Robert's Source Texts folder as the source of the Babcock descent.
- 1778 note: Joshua was chosen representative of Westerly in May 1778; his half-brother protested the slave-enlistment act in the RI colonial records.
- Franklin often stayed with Babcock when passing through Westerly (Babcock-Smith House, HABS RI-142).

RELATIONSHIP TO ROBERT (from research log, 2026-09-16 section): Dr. Joshua Babcock (1707-1783) = son of Capt. James Babcock; first cousin of Oliver Babcock Jr. (1722-1806). So he is a COLLATERAL cousin (Robert's 7th-great-grandfather's first cousin), not a direct ancestor.
