Newspaper research on the Pfeffer family of Columbia/Waterloo, Monroe County,
Illinois -- the family that adopted Mary Blackwell (Ann Blackwell's daughter,
b. abt 1877, Oneida County Poorhouse, Rome NY), who grew up as "Johanna Mary"
/ "Jennie" Pfeffer before marrying Jacob Emil Brand in 1904 under her birth
name, Marietta Blackwell.

1) JOHN A. PFEFFER'S DEATH -- The Waterloo Republican, Waterloo, Illinois,
2 September 1908, p.8 ("Neighborhood News" column)

"Mr. and Mrs. Henry Wessell, and Mr. and Mrs. Henry Fischer of New Hanover
came in Friday to attend the Pfeffer burial. John Wiest of Fountain, who
purchased the old Henfling house from Henry Reichenbach, moved his family
into their new home Monday.

"On account of the Pfeffer funeral Friday morning the First National Bank
was closed till noon. Mr. Pfeffer was one of the directors of the bank."

Elsewhere in the same issue, a separate item lists out-of-town mourners:
"Among the many who attended the funeral of John A. Pfeffer last Friday we
mention those from a distance: M. Sullivan, M. J. Walsh, Edward Keshner,
Valentine Hauser of East [St. Louis?], Thomas Koenigsmark, Hartmann, and H.
Gausman of St. Louis; H. Deken of Jacksonville; Dr. H. Heidelberg of Hecker;
Koechel, Chas. Morrison..." [list continues, partially cropped]

A brief local item the same week: "Quite a number of our people went to
Columbia Friday to attend the funeral of John Pfeffer. Mr. Pfeffer was well
known here and held in high esteem by all."

2) MARY/"JENNIE" PFEFFER AS A YOUNG WOMAN -- The Waterloo Republican,
18 May 1904, p.5 ("Local Items" column)

"Miss Jennie Pfeffer of Columbia is the guest of Miss Celia Leibach."

This is a routine one-line social note, but it is a genuine, contemporary
sighting of Mary Blackwell as "Jennie Pfeffer," age about 27, living in
Columbia and moving in local social circles -- just months before her
October/November 1904 marriage to Jacob Brand. ("Jennie" appears to be the
everyday nickname the family used, alongside "Johanna Mary" formally --
matching the 1900 census entry "Jennie Pfeffer, 24, ADOPTED DAUGHTER.")

3) JOHANNA (FUNK) PFEFFER'S OBITUARY (Mary's adoptive mother) -- The
Waterloo Republic-Times, Waterloo, Illinois, 19 November 1915, p.8

"Died, at her home in this city, on Monday, November 15, 1915, Mrs. Johanna
Pfeffer, nee Funk, widow of the late John A. Pfeffer, aged 70 years, 9
months and 5 days. Deceased was born in New Orleans, Feb. 10, 1845. While
still in her teens she came north with her parents and on May 22, 1862, she
was married to John A. Pfeffer, for many years one of our prominent
merchants, who preceded her in death several years ago. To this union were
born eleven children, seven sons and four daughters, all of whom survive and
have grown up. Twenty-eight grand-children also survive. The funeral takes
place in the Catholic cemetery here this (Thursday) morning after requiem
mass in the Catholic church of which deceased was a devout member. The
bereaved relatives have the sincere sympathy of all in the loss of their
dearest friend on earth."

WHAT THIS ADDS TO THE PICTURE OF MARY'S CHILDHOOD

- **The Pfeffers were a prominent, respected local family, not just a
  household that took in an orphan.** John A. Pfeffer was a director of
  Waterloo's First National Bank -- a position of real local standing --
  and "one of our prominent merchants" per his wife's obituary. His funeral
  was significant enough that the bank closed for the morning, and mourners
  came from as far as St. Louis and Jacksonville. Mary/Jennie grew up in a
  household with real social and financial standing in Monroe County, a
  marked contrast to the "Homeless" notation on her 1880 Oneida County
  Poorhouse census record at age 3.

- **Devoutly, actively Catholic.** Johanna's obituary specifies a requiem
  mass and burial in the Catholic cemetery, and calls her "a devout member"
  of the parish. This matches the 1904 record of Mary/Marietta's own
  marriage to Jacob Brand "at the Catholic church, Father H. Goosens
  officiating" (per the existing family record) -- she was raised in, and
  married within, the same Catholic community as her adoptive family.

- **A very large family to grow up inside.** Johanna's 1915 obituary states
  eleven biological children (seven sons, four daughters) plus twenty-eight
  grandchildren surviving by 1915 -- a slight correction to the earlier
  "fourteen biological children" figure from the 1894 biographical sketch
  (either an error in that source, or children who had died between 1894
  and 1915 and so are not counted among the 1915 obituary's survivors).
  Either way, Mary/Jennie was one of at least two adopted children (herself
  and Elizabeth Smith) raised alongside a houseful of Pfeffer-born siblings
  -- a bustling, well-off, socially visible household, not an isolated
  placement.

- **She kept a social presence of her own by her mid-20s.** The 1904 "Miss
  Jennie Pfeffer... is the guest of Miss Celia Leibach" note, however small,
  shows she was an unremarkable, ordinary young woman about town in Columbia
  right up to her marriage -- fully integrated into local social life, using
  the Pfeffer name as her own.

NOT YET FOUND: no newspaper item located so far actually names "Jennie"
or "Mary" or "Johanna Mary" Pfeffer as a child (school honors, First
Communion, etc.) -- only as a young woman shortly before her marriage.
Illinois's own Digital Newspaper Collection has no pre-1939 Columbia, IL
paper, and Newspapers.com's Monroe County holdings for the 1880s-1890s
(when Mary was a young child there) are much thinner than for the 1900s+;
a future pass specifically targeting 1885-1895 Monroe County items, or the
1894 "Portrait & Biographical Record" book directly (already the source of
the core adoption facts), would be the next place to look for that.
