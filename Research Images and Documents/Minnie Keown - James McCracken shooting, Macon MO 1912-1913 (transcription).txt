The "Minnie Keown" case -- a sensational, nationally-syndicated 1912-1913 Macon,
Missouri criminal case. Surfaced via a Newspapers.com search of the free
State Historical Society of Missouri (SHSMO) collection, Adair/Macon County
papers. FLAGGED AS LIKELY UNCONNECTED to this project's documented Keown line
-- see notes at the end.

1) THE SHOOTING -- The Macon Times-Democrat, Macon, Missouri, 4 July 1912, p.1

"A TRAGEDY AT MIDNIGHT -- JAMES MC CRACKEN SHOT BY MINNIE KEOWN -- Woman Says
He Tried to Get in Her House -- Will be Charged With Murder in First Degree."

"Mrs. Minnie Keown, aged 35, shot and killed James McCracken a few minutes
after 12 o'clock Saturday night. The killing occurred near Mrs. Keown's
residence, a half block south of the south end of the Rubey street viaduct.
Mrs. Keown's husband, Jas. L. Keown, died at Meridian, Miss., Saturday
morning, and the widow was awake that night because of agitation over his
death.

"After he was shot, McCracken was placed in an automobile and taken to the
office of Dr. A. M. Raines, where he expired soon afterwards. McCracken
formerly ran a saloon..."

[Adjacent column, apparently reprinted testimony from a later trial:] a
neighbor, "Mr. Reynolds," on being asked to relate to the jury all he knew
about the case: "Well, the first thing I saw was two persons, a woman and a
man, over on the opposite side of the street from my house. The thing that
disturbed me from my sleep was the loud talking. I jumped right up out of
bed, opened the latch of the screen door and went out on the porch. I saw
these two persons somewhere close to the swing, pretty near in front of the
porch. I heard the first shot. That was before I got out on the porch. That
and the loud talking is what woke me up. The two persons whom I saw seemed
to be moving back and forth; this kept going on; as near as I can tell until
they got pretty near to the sidewalk in front of the house and then there
was a shot fired, another one. And just a little bit after that there was
another shot..."

2) THE FIRST TRIAL -- The Macon Times-Democrat, 17 October 1912, p.1

"JURY COULD NOT AGREE -- A MISTRIAL IN THE MINNIE KEOWN CASE -- On Last
Ballot Eight Jurymen Voted for Manslaughter in Second Degree -- Four
Opposed."

"There was a hung jury in the Minnie Keown case. On the last ballot eight
stood for manslaughter in the second degree and four voted 'no' on that
proposition. The first ballot seven voted for acquittal and five for
guilty. The case will be tried again. Mrs. Keown has been released on a
$5,000 bond. The evidence was concluded last..."

Prosecutor's closing argument (same page): "...in the street. When she run
out in the street with a revolver and laid her eyes upon Jim McCracken he
was lost. She was the judge, the jury and the executioner in that case. He
was tried, convicted, sentenced and shot in the time that it took her to
drive him across the street. Did she give him any presumption of innocence
in that case? No! No! Did she convict him beyond a reasonable doubt? No! In
her blind rage she laid upon the first man she saw and shot him down like
she would a rabid animal, and they come in here and tell us that we are not
giving her a fair trial! Now, gentlemen of the jury, I believe this woman is
guilty. I believe she ought to pay the penalty for the crime. I believe this
jury..." [text continues off the saved clip]

3) THE SECOND TRIAL / ACQUITTAL -- Omaha World-Herald, 7 January 1913, p.2
(also carried in the St. Louis Republic, Belleville News-Democrat, Sioux
City Journal, and other papers as far away as Indiana on 6 Jan 1913)

"MRS. MINNIE KEOWN ACQUITTED OF MURDER

"Macon, Mo., Jan. 6. -- Mrs. Minnie Keown was acquitted of the charge of
murdering James McCracken at her second trial, that ended here when a jury
filed a verdict at midnight Saturday. Mrs. Keown's first trial was last
September, resulting in a 'hung jury.'

"McCracken was killed at the woman's home on the night of June 30, 1911
[sic -- almost certainly a typo for 1912 in this wire reprint; every
contemporaneous 1912 source places the shooting in late June 1912, matching
her husband's death the same night]. Mrs. Keown, who was a widow, had been
annoyed by intruders, and when McCracken knocked for admission she fired
through the door with a pistol."

SUMMARY OF THE STORY

Minnie Keown, a 35-year-old Macon, Missouri widow, learned on the morning of
Saturday, 29 June 1912 that her husband, "Jas. L. Keown," had died in
Meridian, Mississippi. Agitated and unable to sleep that night, she was
apparently alone at her home near the Rubey Street viaduct when a man named
James McCracken (a former saloon-keeper) came to her door after midnight.
By her own account she had been "annoyed by intruders" before, and when he
knocked for admission she fired a pistol through the door, killing him.

She was charged with first-degree murder and held without bail. Her first
trial (September/October 1912) ended in a hung jury -- on the final ballot,
8 jurors favored convicting her of second-degree manslaughter and 4 opposed
even that; the prosecutor's closing argument (excerpted above) argued she
had acted as "judge, jury and executioner." Her second trial concluded with
a full acquittal, verdict returned at midnight Saturday, 4 January 1913.
Coverage of the story reached at least Missouri, Kansas, Nebraska,
Oklahoma, Illinois, Indiana, and California papers -- genuinely
nationally syndicated for a local case, presumably because of its
dramatic "widow shoots man at her door the night her husband dies elsewhere"
premise.

NOTES ON CONNECTION TO THIS PROJECT'S DOCUMENTED KEOWN TREE:

This is almost certainly NOT the same Keown family as Nathan Lusk Keown /
Nathan Jackson Keown / Thomas Henderson Keown documented elsewhere in this
project. Reasons:

- No "James L. Keown" (Minnie's husband) appears anywhere among Nathan
  Jackson Keown's documented children (John Henry, Thomas Henderson,
  William Bedford, Phoebe E., Mary E., Nancy E., and three children who died
  in the 1842 smallpox outbreak) or among Thomas Henderson Keown's six
  children (all daughters).
- This project already identified and ruled out one other, unrelated,
  same-surname Keown family in this exact area: "a same-named, unrelated
  Nathan J. Keown (b. Scotland, d. 1889 Macon MO) -- coincidental namesake,
  not ours" (session note, searching "Nathan Keown" nationally). Minnie and
  James L. Keown of Macon, MO in 1912 are very plausibly connected to that
  same already-known, separate Macon County Keown family rather than to our
  line, which was centered in Adair County (Kirksville) before splitting to
  Nebraska (William B., Nathan) and Arkansas/California (Thomas H.) --
  not into Macon County.
- No further identifying detail (parents, siblings, where "Jas. L. Keown"
  himself came from) was pursued beyond what's transcribed above, since the
  weight of evidence already points away from a connection. If a future
  session wants to pin down "Jas. L. Keown" definitively (e.g. via a Macon
  County MO death/probate record, or his own obituary), that would be the
  next step -- but on current evidence this is flagged as an unconnected,
  same-surname family, kept on file because it's a genuinely well-documented
  and dramatic Missouri Keown-surname story from the same general region and
  era.
