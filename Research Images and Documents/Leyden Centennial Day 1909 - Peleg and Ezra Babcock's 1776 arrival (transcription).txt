"Leyden's Centennial Day" -- coverage of Leyden, Massachusetts's 100th-anniversary
celebration, including a historical paper on the town's founding read by local
historian C. Wells Severance. This is the source for Peleg Babcock's documented
1776 arrival in Leyden from Rhode Island.

Source: Springfield Weekly Republican (Springfield, Mass.), 19 August 1909, p. 12.

Relevant excerpt, from C. Wells Severance's historical paper on Leyden (as printed):

"Leyden is a small town situated in the northern part of Franklin county
contiguous to the state of Vermont, bounded on the north by the state line, east
by Bernardston, south by Greenfield and west by Colrain, containing about 9684
acres with a population of 363 in 1905, and had 1000 in 1809. Originally a part
of Bernardston it was made a separate district in 1784. The first district or
town-meeting was held April 26, 1784, Caleb Adams serving the warrant. It
remained a district till 1809, when it became a town. About the year 1773 we
find the first settlers in this section which were Enoch Briggs, Charles Packer
and Mrs Frizzell, Enoch Briggs settling where J. H. Newcomb, Jr., lives, Charles
Packer where George Williams lives and Mrs Frizzell on East hill. These three
with one built where Mr Doolittle lives were the first four framed houses built
in town. In 1775 Elder Joseph Green, great-grandfather of Albert Green came from
Rhode Island and settled on the old county road, south of Childs Brown's place.
In the following year Paul Green, Albert's grandfather, Peleg and Ezra Babcock
came from the same place. Peleg settling where Charles Robertson lives, hence
the name Pelegs' Bog, and Ezra settling where Mrs R. B. Robertson lives. Charles
Clark settled about the same time where Eli Frary lives and set out the first
apple orchard in the dark day which occured in 1780.

"The first person born was Polly, daughter of Richard and Patience Grinnell,
where J. H. Newcomb lives. The first marriage was in 1784, that of Caleb Adams
and Catherine Davenport, where Sidney Lamphear lives.

"The first tavern and store was kept by Thomas Wells, on the old road, south of
C. F. Severance, the second by Paul Babcock, south of J. H. Newcomb's, and the
third by Fuller in Beaver meadow."

NOTES:

- This dates Peleg [W.] Babcock's arrival in Leyden to 1776, from Rhode Island --
  earlier than previously documented in this project, and gives a specific place
  name, "Pelegs' Bog," still recognized locally as of 1909.
- Names a previously unknown "Ezra Babcock" who arrived with Peleg the same year
  -- likely a brother or close relative, not yet identified against Oliver
  Babcock Jr.'s known children.
- Confirms Paul Babcock (Peleg's brother, per this project's existing research --
  Oliver Babcock Jr.'s other son) kept "the second tavern and store" in early
  Leyden.
- Elsewhere in the same issue's coverage, a separate item notes the old local
  mail system: "the pack of papers was labeled Peleg Babcock, with each
  subscriber's name on his paper" -- confirming Peleg's standing as a recognized
  local landowner/subscriber.
