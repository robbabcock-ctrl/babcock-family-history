1) FULL TRANSCRIPTION — "Portrait and Biographical Record of Randolph,
Jackson, Perry and Monroe Counties, Illinois" (Biographical Publishing Co.,
Chicago, 1894), pp. 411-412. Source: archive.org/details/portraitbiographi00biograp,
leaves 403-404 (also independently transcribed by the Jackson County
ILGenWeb project, jackson.illinoisgenweb.org/biographies/pfefferjohn.html,
confirmed word-for-word against the original page image).

"JOHN A. PFEFFER is a worthy representative of the business interests of
Columbia, where he is engaged in general merchandising. As he is widely and
favorably known in this community, we feel assured that the record of his
life will prove of interest to many of our readers. He was born in St. Clair
County, Ill., August 1, 1844, and is of German descent. His father, John
Pfeffer, was born and reared in the city of Candel [Kandel], Bavaria,
Germany, and in 1832 came with his parents to America, the family landing in
New Orleans, where the grandfather of our subject died. In 1840 his wife,
with her five children, came to Illinois. One of the sisters remained in the
Crescent City, and there married Fred Eckel. The family settled on a farm in
Centreville Township, St. Clair County, and John Pfeffer and his brother
cleared and improved the land, transforming it into a fine farm. They also
engaged in steamboating on the Mississippi between St. Louis and New
Orleans.

"In the latter place the father of our subject married Margaret Scherrer, a
native of Lorraine, France, who came to America in 1836. Mr. Pfeffer
afterwards bought a farm of partially improved land in Monroe County and
continued its cultivation for a year. He then went to St. Louis, where he
worked about five years as an engineer in a distillery. From 1852 until 1856
he again carried on his farm, and then came with his brother-in-law, F. X.
Brohammer, to Columbia, where they conducted a saloon and mercantile
business. In 1857 the stock was divided, and Mr. Pfeffer continued alone in
trade until 1890, when he sold out to his son. His death occurred on the
16th of October following, and his wife died October 12, 1892. They were
parents of fourteen children, but only two are now living, John A., of this
sketch, and Mrs. Catherine Dollar, of Columbia. In politics Mr. Pfeffer was
a Democrat, and was Trustee of his town. He held membership with the Roman
Catholic Church. He was a successful business man, and at his death left an
estate valued at $40,000.

"Our subject was a lad of twelve when his father established business in
Columbia. He clerked in the store until his marriage, when his father gave
him the saloon, which he carried on until 1890, when he bought out the
mercantile establishment. He has since carried on operations along that
line, and by well directed efforts and good management has secured a fine
trade. He now occupies a large store, which is well stocked with everything
found in his line.

"May 22, 1866 [see note below re: likely 1862], Mr. Pfeffer was united in
marriage with Miss Johanna K. Funk, daughter of John Funk, of St. Clair
County. Her father was a native of Bavaria, Germany, and in 1845 came to the
New World, locating in St. Clair County. Mrs. Pfeffer was born in New
Orleans, February 10, 1845. By their marriage they had fourteen children, of
whom the following are yet living: John Joseph, who is clerking for his
father; Joseph F., who is studying medicine in St. Louis; E. A. F., Peter
A., Mary, Theodore, Annie; Louis and Katie, twins, and Charlie. Mary Ann
died at the age of three years, John at the age of ten months, and Louis at
the age of nine days. John Joseph married Bertha Reis, a daughter of Charles
P. Reis.

"In his political views, Mr. Pfeffer is a Democrat, and has been a member
of the Village Board of Trustees, while for eleven years he served as a
member of the School Board. In his undertakings he has met with prosperity,
and in connection with the interests previously mentioned, he has a farm of
two hundred and ninety-three acres that yields to him a good income. His
home is a commodious and substantial brick residence. Mr. and Mrs. Pfeffer
are noted for their kindliness, and through their generosity gave homes to
two adopted children: Elizabeth Smith, who became the wife of Frank L.
Riebeling, a wagonmaker of Ava, Jackson County, Ill.; and Johanna Mary, who
is still living with them. Our subject, his wife and children are all
members of the Roman Catholic Church."

2) CIVIC LIFE — NEWSPAPER CONFIRMATIONS BEYOND THE 1894 SKETCH

- **First National Bank of Waterloo, director.** Confirmed by his funeral
  coverage: "On account of the Pfeffer funeral Friday morning the First
  National Bank was closed till noon. Mr. Pfeffer was one of the directors
  of the bank." (Waterloo Republican, 2 Sep 1908.)

- **Columbia Gymnastic Society ("Turnverein"), president, then trustee.**
  Waterloo Republic-Times, 12 Nov 1896: "The annual meeting of the Columbia
  Gymnastic Society took place at their hall last Thursday night. The
  meeting was opened by John A. Pfeffer, president... The following were
  selected as trustees: John A. Pfeffer, Henry Reichenbach jr. and Nick
  Schoppe." A German-American social/athletic society (gymnastic instructor,
  janitor, financier's report on cash-on-hand of $194.15) -- consistent with
  the family's Bavarian roots and Columbia's substantial German immigrant
  community.

- **Columbia creamery company, organizing secretary and stock commissioner.**
  Waterloo Republican, 25 Mar 1897: "A mass meeting of farmers and others was
  held at the town hall last Monday to discuss the advisability of building
  a creamery... Ed. Schoening was chosen chairman and J. A. Pfeffer
  secretary... the following three commissioners were appointed to solicit
  subscriptions of stock: J. A. Pfeffer, Frank Hermann, and Wm. Menner. The
  capital stock was fixed at $3,000, divided into 120 shares of $25 each."
  Shows him active in local agricultural/economic development beyond his own
  store, fitting his 293-acre farm holding.

- **Election judge.** Waterloo Republican, 29 Sep 1898, list of "judges of
  election" selected for Monroe County precincts: "Poll 2 at Ritter's -- Jos
  Arnin, [J.] A. Pfeffer, C. McMullen." A routine but genuine mark of local
  civic trust.

- **Village Board of Trustees and School Board (11 years)** -- per the 1894
  sketch above; no additional Village Board or School Board meeting minutes
  specifically naming him were located in this pass (Columbia's own paper,
  the Columbia Star, is not digitized on Newspapers.com before the 1920s, so
  Columbia's own local-government coverage from his lifetime is largely
  inaccessible; Waterloo's papers cover Waterloo's own village government in
  far more detail than Columbia's).

- **"Catholic Knights of Illinois" membership** -- noted in an earlier pass
  of this project's research but NOT independently re-confirmed with a
  citation in this session; flagged as unverified pending a future source
  check, not restated here as settled fact.

3) HIS OWN BUSINESS -- SUMMARY OF THE CAREER ARC

1844: born (St. Clair Co., IL per his own 1894 sketch; see birthplace
discrepancy note below).
~1856 (age 12): father established the general store in Columbia; John A.
began clerking there.
~1862 or 1866 (marriage): father gave him the SALOON side of the business to
run in his own right.
1862-1890: ran the saloon.
1890: bought out his father's MERCANTILE establishment (the store) outright;
his father retired from trade the same year and died that October.
1890-1908: ran the general store himself, described in 1894 as "a large
store, which is well stocked with everything found in his line," having
"secured a fine trade" through "well directed efforts and good management."
Also held a 293-acre farm and was a director of Waterloo's First National
Bank.

No direct advertisement for his own Columbia store was located in this pass
-- Columbia's own newspaper isn't digitized for this era, and the
surviving Waterloo papers (which do cover Columbia's social/civic news
extensively) mostly carry ads for Waterloo's own merchants rather than
Columbia's. This remains an open item for a future session if Columbia-
specific advertising ever surfaces.

4) DEATH -- A GENUINE TRAGEDY, NOT A QUIET OLD-AGE PASSING

Full transcription, Belleville Daily Advocate, Belleville, Illinois,
Wednesday, 26 August 1908, p. 1:

"DIED OF CHLOROFORM
John A. Pfeffer, Columbia, Ill., Merchant, Succumbs to Chloroform While
Undergoing Operation.

"Belleville relatives of John A. Pfeffer, aged 62 years, a well-to-do
retired merchant of Columbia, Ill., received word Tuesday afternoon of his
death on an operating table at a Paducah, Ky., hospital. Mr. Pfeffer, while
on a visit to Creal Springs, Ill., was thrown from a buggy in a runaway. He
received a fracture of the hip. His relatives state that he died while he
was under the influence of chloroform, undergoing the setting of the
fractured hip.

"The body passed through East St. Louis Tuesday en route to Columbia. His
St. Louis relatives met the train in East St. Louis. Pfeffer's St. Louis
relatives are three sons, Dr. Peter A. Pfeffer, 3940 Shaw avenue; Dr.
Francis Joseph Pfeffer, 1653 South Jefferson avenue; and Theodore J. Pfeffer
of Ashland avenue. Other relatives live in Belleville, Lebanon, Nauvoo,
Jacksonville and other points in Illinois. His widow, Mrs. Johanna Pfeffer,
and several children lived with him at his home in Columbia, Ill. Francis
Joseph, Theodore J., of St. Louis, and John J., who lives in Lebanon, went
to Columbia on the train that carried the body.

"John A. Pfeffer was born at Third and Market streets in St. Louis
sixty-two years ago."

WHAT HAPPENED: Pfeffer, then 62 and already a retired merchant, was
visiting Creal Springs, Illinois (a small resort town in Williamson County,
southern Illinois, known at the time for mineral springs) when the horse
pulling his buggy bolted and threw him, breaking his hip. He was taken to a
hospital across the Ohio River in Paducah, Kentucky for the fracture to be
set -- and died on the operating table under chloroform anesthesia, a real
and far-from-rare risk of the era's crude anesthetic practice. His body was
brought home by train through East St. Louis, met there by his St. Louis-
based sons, and continued on to Columbia for burial.

TWO SONS BECAME PHYSICIANS IN ST. LOUIS: Dr. Peter A. Pfeffer (3940 Shaw
Avenue) and Dr. Francis Joseph Pfeffer (1653 South Jefferson Avenue) --
confirming and extending the 1894 sketch's note that "Joseph F.... is
studying medicine in St. Louis." A third son, Theodore J., also lived in
St. Louis (Ashland Avenue); son John J. lived in Lebanon, Illinois.

BIRTHPLACE DISCREPANCY -- genuinely unresolved: the 1894 biographical
sketch (presumably drawn from an interview with Pfeffer himself, in his
prime, for a vanity county history) states he was born in **St. Clair
County, Illinois**. This 1908 obituary (drawn from family/relatives' account
after his sudden death) states he was born **"at Third and Market streets in
St. Louis."** These are two different claims from two independently good
sources -- not a simple typo, since one names a rural Illinois county and
the other a specific urban St. Louis intersection. No way to resolve this
with what's been found so far; flagged rather than silently picking one.
Worth checking a St. Louis or St. Clair County birth/baptismal record if one
can ever be located for 1 Aug 1844.

MARRIAGE DATE DISCREPANCY -- likely resolved: the 1894 sketch gives "May 22,
1866" for his marriage to Johanna K. Funk, but Johanna's own 1915 obituary
gives "May 22, 1862." The 1900 census entry for the household records the
couple as married 38 years as of 1900 -- exactly matching 1862, not 1866.
Treating 1862 as correct; the 1894 book's "1866" is most likely a
typesetting/transcription error for "1862."

5) FAMILY SUMMARY (per the 1894 sketch, corroborated where possible)

Wife: Johanna K. Funk (10 Feb 1845, New Orleans -- 15 Nov 1915, Columbia,
IL), daughter of John Funk (b. Bavaria, emigrated to St. Clair Co. IL 1845).

Fourteen children born; by 1894 the following were living: John Joseph
(clerking for his father; m. Bertha Reis), Joseph F. [= Dr. Francis Joseph,
by 1908], E. A. F., Peter A. [= Dr. Peter A., by 1908], Mary, Theodore [=
Theodore J.], Annie, Louis and Katie (twins), and Charlie. Three had died in
childhood: Mary Ann (age 3), John (age 10 months), and an earlier Louis
(age 9 days) -- the twin "Louis" born later evidently given the same name as
this deceased infant brother, a common 19th-century practice. By Johanna's
1915 obituary, eleven children (seven sons, four daughters) survived her,
with 28 grandchildren.

Two adopted children, raised alongside the above: Elizabeth Smith (m. Frank
L. Riebeling, a wagonmaker of Ava, Jackson County, IL) and Johanna Mary --
our own Mary "Marietta" Blackwell, who married Jacob Emil Brand in 1904.
