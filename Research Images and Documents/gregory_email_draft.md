# Draft email to Jim Gregory (awaiting Robert's approval + confirmed address)

**Subject: Thomas H. Keown of the 12th Missouri Cavalry — from his great-great-great-grandson**

Dear Mr. Gregory,

My name is Robert Babcock, and Thomas Henderson Keown — the old Powder River cavalryman
whose photograph and gravestone you shared in your September post, "To the Historians of the
Powder River Country, from Central California" — was my great-great-great-grandfather. My
grandmother, Marjorie Schultz Rawson, was one of the grandchildren at the Arroyo Grande ranch;
she remembered sitting in his lap as a little girl and running her fingers through that white
beard, and she passed his story down to me.

I've been researching the family seriously, and I wanted first to thank you. Your work gave
our family things we did not have: the 1935 Herald-Recorder page with the photograph of Thomas
and Phoebe on their fiftieth anniversary, Phoebe's own essay on the Grand Army's motto, and
the knowledge that his comrade James Dowell rests near him.

I'd also like to offer you what we've turned up recently, in case any of it is new to your
files:

- Missouri State Archives service cards showing that Thomas's father, **Sgt. Nathan J. Keown,
  Co. B, 21st Missouri Infantry**, enlisted July 6, 1861 in Adair County alongside Thomas's
  older brother, **Cpl. John H. Keown**, then about sixteen. John died of disease in the
  hospital at Hannibal on April 1, 1862 — five days before his regiment opened the Battle of
  Shiloh. Nathan was discharged for disability at Mound City in January 1863. (You may know
  Nathan already — he was a charter member of the Colonel Harper Post in 1880, seventeen
  years before Thomas arrived in Arroyo Grande.)
- Thomas's pension increase was carried in an act of Congress: Act of January 27, 1911,
  ch. 30, at 36 Stat. 1899 — "the name of Thomas H. Keown, late of Company F, Twelfth
  Regiment Missouri Volunteer Cavalry... twenty-four dollars per month."

And I have questions I hope you can help with, as the person who has looked hardest at these
men:

1. In *Patriot Graves* and your articles, you describe Thomas surrounded during the Powder
   River expedition — fighting from behind his dead horse, and a rescue. What were your
   sources for that account? (A newspaper reminiscence? A county history? The 1935
   Herald-Recorder?) I'd love to cite them properly in the family history I'm writing.
2. Do you have other Keown material — clippings on his G.A.R. years, the 1933 post
   dissolution, obituaries, or photographs of him, Phoebe, or Nathan?
3. Do you know what became of the records of the Fred Steele and Colonel Harper posts when
   they closed?
4. Do you know whether Nathan is buried in the Arroyo Grande District Cemetery or elsewhere
   in the county? His death and grave are my biggest open question.
5. And of course — I'd be glad to buy *Patriot Graves* directly from you or the Historical
   Society if copies remain.

Thank you for keeping these soldiers' memory alive. My grandmother would have loved knowing
that a historian in Arroyo Grande was still telling her grandfather's story.

With appreciation,
Robert Babcock
