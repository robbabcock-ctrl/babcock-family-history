# Ann Blackwell — The "Mrs. David Owens" Lead
**Research session notes · August 31, 2026**
Companion to: *The Girl from the Poorhouse* (Babcock family history paper, Aug 2026)

---

## The record in question

Ann Blackwell's second admission to the Oneida County Almshouse (No. 4115, adm. Mar. 16, 1905, "very feeble," last residence Utica) lists under **Relatives or Friends**:

> Mrs. David Owens, 305 Sunset Ave., Utica, N.Y.

The family paper floated the possibility this was a married sister. This session developed a competing — and currently stronger — hypothesis.

## Working hypothesis: employer, not sister

**Mrs. David Owens was the wife of Ann's employer, and 305 Sunset Ave. was the house Ann served in — possibly the same family she served for much of her working life.**

Reasoning developed this session:

1. **The "relatives or friends" line on almshouse admissions was commonly the last household an aging domestic served in.** Employers frequently arranged the admission of servants who could no longer work; the keeper recorded whoever brought her or could be contacted.
2. **Surname/ethnicity mismatch weakens the sister theory.** Owens in 1905 Utica is an overwhelmingly Welsh, Protestant surname; Ann was Irish Catholic. Also, in 1876 Ann told the poorhouse "Relatives: None." Not impossible she had a sister, but the employer reading requires fewer assumptions.
3. **Naming the *wife* thirty years later suggests a long, close tie** — employers rarely remained the contact for a servant who merely passed through.

## Extension: the paternity angle (Scott's theory)

Timeline is consistent with concealment of an employer pregnancy:
- Admitted Sept. 30, 1876 — roughly 4 months pregnant, at the edge of showing. The right moment to leave a household quietly.
- Delivered Feb. 2, 1877, in the poorhouse; Mary placed at House of the Good Shepherd, Utica.
- Superintendent's note: for her first child she **swore paternity** on "a boy by the name of Martin," who paid ~$20 support through the County Superintendent. For Mary, "she can not swear who is its father."
- **Key inference:** Ann demonstrably knew how to swear paternity and had done it successfully. Declining to do so the second time reads as *couldn't afford to name him* at least as plausibly as *didn't know*. A married employer is exactly the father a servant couldn't name without losing position, reference, and support.

**Honest counterweights (keep in the file):**
- The poorhouse was the default maternity ward for poor unmarried women in 1876 — admission alone proves poverty, not concealment.
- Returning "very feeble" in 1905 was the standard end for aging domestics without family; consistent with the theory, not unique to it.
- The 20-year "disappearance" between poorhouse records most likely = ordinary self-supporting service work, 1877–1905.

## What this session established about 305 Sunset Ave.

- **The 300 block of Sunset Ave. no longer exists.** Current numbering starts around the 800–900s. Lower blocks were renumbered or taken — dozens of West Utica homes were demolished for the North–South Arterial (Rte. 12), first built in the 1960s and reconstructed ~2012–2016; state archaeologists excavated house lots on Sunset Ave. (e.g., 1019 Sunset) during the project. The house is almost certainly gone.
- West Utica was a working-class immigrant district; Utica held one of the largest Welsh communities in America — consistent with an Owens household there.
- Nearby hospitals (Faxton, St. Luke's, St. Elizabeth's) — relevant if any record shows a hospital death/residence split.

## Research plan — in priority order

1. **1900 federal census: David Owens, Utica, Oneida Co.** Gives his age (→ tests the 1876 falsifier), marriage year, occupation, and whether a servant lived in. If Ann (b. Ireland, imm. ~1865, single, servant) is in the household → near-confirmation of the employment link.
2. **1905 NY State census (taken June 1905).** Lists street + house number. Owens household should appear at 305 Sunset (West Utica ward); Ann should appear in the Rome almshouse enumeration. Ancestry: "New York, State Census, 1905."
3. **Walk Ann backward: 1900 → 1892 NY state census → 1880 federal.** In 1880 she'd be ~34, servant, born Ireland, with 3-year-old Mary at Good Shepherd. Same-household hits across decades = "worked there her whole life" proven.
4. **Utica city directories (FREE, already located).** Utica Public Library scans: uticapubliclibrary.org → Resources → Genealogy → Utica City Directories. PDFs hosted at uplinside.org (blocks bots — download manually and upload to Claude to have them read).
   - **1905 volume is a STREET directory** — organized by address; will show the 305 Sunset household directly. Compiled within months of Ann's admission.
   - Also pull 1902, 1906, 1907 to bracket; 1913 Polk directory is free full-text on Internet Archive (item: uticanewyorkcity00unse) to test persistence.
   - Directories often listed live-in servants at the employer's address: look for "Blackwell, Ann, domestic."
5. **Bastardy/paternity file for the FIRST child.** Martin's ~$20 support ran through the County Superintendent → possible bastardy bond or overseer-of-the-poor filing at Oneida County records, naming Martin in full and possibly stating where Ann lived/worked in the early 1870s. Contact: Oneida County History Center, Utica (lookups for small fee; also holds Sanborn fire maps showing the 305 Sunset house footprint).
6. **Ann's death record** (she died at the almshouse, after Mar. 1905). Discharge/death notation should be on the same register reel (NYSA Series A1978, reel A1978:150). State death certificate filed through Rome; NY State death index 1880–1956 (Ancestry) → certificate number → order from Rome city clerk or NYS DOH. **Best remaining chance the record names her parents.**
7. Standing items from the paper: Mary's 1877 Catholic baptism in Rome, N.Y. (may name Ann's Irish home parish); House of the Good Shepherd placement file.


## Status of the theory

Plausible, motivated by real features of the record, and **testable** — it predicts Ann appears in the Owens (or one family's) household across multiple censuses, and that David Owens was a married householder by 1876. Not yet evidenced; the 1900 census pull is the hinge.
