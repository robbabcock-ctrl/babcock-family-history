Three letters written from Arroyo Grande, California, by Nathan J. Keown and his wife
Matilda (Nicholson) Keown, to Matilda's sister Mrs. Sarah Shontz of Memphis, Missouri.

These are the letters Robert remembered: Matilda's early letters praising California as a
kind of paradise, and Nathan's final letter announcing her death.

PROVENANCE (per the 1951/1976 editor's note): The letters were preserved among Sarah
Shontz's personal effects. Sarah later moved to Ely, Nevada; after her death, her grandson
Casey Jones (employed by Howard and Bates plumbing) found the letters and gave them to
Edgar Bates, a local collector of old envelopes/covers and stamps. Bates made them available
to the Arroyo Grande Herald-Recorder, which published them as "Letters from Yesteryear" on
5 October 1951. The Times-Press-Recorder reprinted the piece on 29 September 1976, p. 90,
which is the copy this transcription is drawn from.

Source: Times-Press-Recorder, Arroyo Grande, CA, 29 Sep 1976, p. 90 ("Letters from
Yesteryear," reprinted from the Arroyo Grande Herald-Recorder, 5 Oct 1951). Found via the
California Digital Newspaper Collection (California State Library / UC Riverside),
persistent link: https://cdnc.ucr.edu/?a=d&d=FCTPR19760929.1.90
(Not on Newspapers.com -- that service's digitized run of the Arroyo Grande paper only
starts December 1889, after Nathan had already returned to Nebraska.)

LETTER 1 -- written by Matilda Keown, Sept. 9, 1885

"I take pen in hand to let you know that we still live, are in modest health...in the
first place we left Nebraska on 28 April; got to San Francisco May 5...took steamer, came
to Cambria; from there to Arroyo Grande. Bought a house, one acre of land; house and shop;
stable; stove, two tables, two bedsteads, two bureaus, safe; two cows; and soon paid $900
for all. Lots of garden, and well. We have vegetables all of the time, and fruit, and lots
of fish. We have a nice buggy and pony; live four miles from the beach. Have a nice time
fishing. The old man (her husband) likes it first rate. The climate is nice, no thunder
storms, no lightning, no cyclones, no rain since we came. I like the people first rate all
friendly and clever (meaning accommodating -- Ed.). Good schools and churches. People take
lots of comfort camping out, hunting and fishing. This is a nice little valley and very rich
land, hilly and broken. It grows fine wheat, barley and oats, potatoes and fruit, and beans.
They call this year a failure in crops. Times are hard here. Well, Sarah, you said we would
not like it. We like it quite well so far. All I am sorry for is, we did not come sooner...
Love to all
Ma (Matilda)
to Sarah and Cap."

(On the back of the sheet, her husband Nathan wrote:)
"The old lady has left some space. I will say we are all as well as common...This is the
finest climate that I was ever in. The Summer has been just as pleasant as it could be and
people tell me that the Fall and Winter will be just like the Summer only that when the
rainy season comes on, we will have some light frost once in awhile. All hardy vegetables
grow all Winter. Tomato vines have been known to grow all Winter, and even two or three
years, so you can see the climate is good enough. We are out of the hot, sultry weather,
also the extreme cold, no chills and fever here. I think it is a very healthy country
here..."

=====================================================================

LETTER 2 -- written by Matilda Keown, Nov. 29, 1886

"...It is time to plant garden now. Frost enough to kill tomato vines...one rain last
month...Crops were good. Beans went to 60 sacks to the acre; one man grew $150 worth of
carrots to the acre; (one) pumpkin 250 pounds; one cabbage 96 pounds; onion, three pounds.
This is the nicest climate I ever saw no Winter or hot weather. The Winter is the nicest
part of the season..."

(On the back, her husband again wrote in part:)
"We live four miles from the beach, a nice road when the tide is down. It is the nicest
place to buggy ride that I've ever seen. We can hear the sea roar out here..."

=====================================================================

LETTER 3 -- written by Nathan J. Keown, Nov. 16, 1888 (the letter is edged in black)

"My Dear Friends:
I seat myself to say that your sister and aunt and my darling and loving wife has gone to
her long home. She departed this life on the 14th...She had been unwell five weeks. I have
been very stout until now. I have sunk...She is better off than I am but my loneliness is
more than I can bear...Write soon and often.
Nathan J. Keown"

=====================================================================

NOTES:

- Matilda died 14 Nov 1888 in Arroyo Grande, matching her death notice in the San Luis
  Obispo Tribune (20 Nov 1888) and her Find A Grave record.
- The 28 April 1885 departure from Nebraska matches Nathan's own 1896 obituary (The Ord
  Quiz), which says he moved to California in April 1885.
- "Sarah and Cap" -- Sarah Shontz and (presumably) her husband, referred to by a nickname
  or initial ("Cap").
- These letters are strong evidence against the claim (made in two 1933 Arroyo Grande
  newspaper pieces) that Nathan was a charter member of Colonel Harper GAR Post since its
  1880 founding -- his wife's own first letter home describes their arrival as brand new
  in 1885.
